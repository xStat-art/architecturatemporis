Why this document

You are taking part in a study of the buffer zone of the UNESCO World Heritage property “Natural and Culturo-Historical Region of Kotor”. Your task is to collect field data on historic buildings inside the assigned zone using a single protocol, so that results from different researchers can be compared and combined into one overall assessment.

The data you collect feeds the ATRC methodology called Chronity. Section 2 explains what it is and why the measurements are designed the way they are.

Important before you go out. Read the whole document at least once. Some parameters call for judgement, not only instrument readings, and without understanding the logic of the method three people will give different scores to the same building. Every site is measured by three researchers independently of each other (section 8), and in the study zone almost all houses stand behind fences, so every measurement is marked with an access level (section 7).

What is Chronity

Chronity is the process by which readable traces of time accumulate and are preserved in a building. The Chronity Index shows what level that process has reached at the moment of measurement. Time alone adds no value: what matters is whether the traces have survived and whether they can be read. This is about legibility, not only the documented date of construction: whether the original texture has survived, whether traces of different eras are visible, whether the building is perceived as a living part of its setting.

The number is called the Chronity Index (CI):

CI = (PI / 25 × 0.40) + (AD × 0.35) + (II / 100 × 0.25)
PI
Perceptual Index A composite sensory score: how far the traces of time are perceived by sight, touch, hearing and smell, regardless of documents. It is made up of six parameters measured on site: A1, PMI, VQI, B2, C1, C2.
AD
Authenticity Density The share of unaltered original material on the visually perceived surfaces of the building, by type of material (section 5.7).
II
Integrity Index The integrity of the original structure of the site and how recoverable it is, on a scale of 0–100. Replacing material in an opening counts towards AD, not II.

The system also applies the PSM (Protection Status Multiplier): 1.0 with no protection status, 1.2 national register, 1.5 site within the boundaries or buffer zone of UNESCO. The multiplier is not calculated by you: you record the protection status of the site in the field (section 6).

Equipment

Bring a smartphone with internet access and location services switched on: the data collection form is filled in on site and picks up GPS coordinates automatically. The phone is also needed for photographs.

For sound (A1) we recommend measuring the level with a sound meter app: an exact decibel value improves the quality of the parameter. For iPhone, NIOSH Sound Level Meter is suitable: free, no ads, checked against standards (accuracy about ±2 dB(A)). There is no equally verified app for Android. In an independent 2025 study, among the free apps Sound Meter and SPL Meter dB (KTW) and Sound Meter (Pony AI) performed better than the others, but accuracy drops at high levels. If you cannot measure, judge by ear using the scale. A notebook is useful if the signal at the site is unstable and you have to fill in the form later from memory.

Measurement parameters

Each parameter is marked R (calculated: a measurement or a clear observation) or E (expert: requires judgement). For E parameters, rely on the scale descriptors; there is no need to agree your score with anyone (section 8).

A1 Sound level calculated

How quiet it is at the site at the moment of measurement. A quiet environment is usually linked to well-preserved historic fabric (little traffic, little modern infrastructure nearby). By decibel range if you have a sound meter, or by ear, by category, without an instrument.

5≤ 40 dB. Deep silence. Only natural sounds, no traffic audible.
441–50 dB. Quiet. Occasional distant traffic, possibly birdsong.
351–60 dB. Moderate. Constant background noise, conversation is comfortable.
261–70 dB. Noticeable urban noise. You have to speak louder.
1> 70 dB. High noise level. The historic atmosphere cannot be sensed.

Instrument. iPhone: NIOSH Sound Level Meter (free, no ads, App Store). Android: there is no NIOSH-verified app. In an independent 2025 study, among the free apps Sound Meter and SPL Meter dB (publisher KTW) and Sound Meter (Pony AI) performed better than the others: find them in Google Play by name. At high noise levels accuracy drops.

Level B. A1 cannot be assessed from the street: give the neutral score 3 (section 7).

PMI Patina Maturity Index expert

How far the surface of the material shows traces of time accumulated naturally (darkening, weathering, biological growth), as opposed to a fresh or artificially aged surface.

5Exceptional. Layering of deposits from different periods, a biological component (moss, lichen), the surface is perceived as alive.
4Mature. Differentiation by surface relief, readable depth, possibly moss in the joints.
3Forming. Uniform change of the original colour, no differentiation of relief, no biological component.
2New or neutral. Original material with no traces of time, uniform colour.
1Cleaned or damaged. Sand-blasted or chemically cleaned, no layer of patina visible.

Types C and D. For surfaces of type C and D (section 5.7) give PMI = 3 as the neutral value: patina does not accumulate on such materials (Appendix J, §J.4). This rule applies at any access level.

VQI Visual Quality Index expert

It is made up of three separately assessed components, each on a scale of 1 to 5; the system calculates the final value (the mean, rounded to 0.5) automatically.

Historical depth of the field of view

5Several distinct historical layers visible at the same time.
4Two clearly distinguishable periods visible together.
3One dominant period, limited layering.
2Layering weak or ambiguous, perception almost single-period.
1No layering visible: a flat, single-moment impression.

Spatial coherence

5The field of view is entirely unified, with no sharp breaks.
4Mostly coherent, minor inconsistencies.
3Mixed: there are both coherent and fragmented zones.
2Noticeably fragmented, several separate zones.
1Entirely fragmented, no sense of wholeness.

Visual pollution (the higher the score, the less pollution)

5No anachronistic elements.
4Minimal. One minor element.
3Moderate. Several elements, but they do not dominate.
2Significant. Elements interfere with perception.
1Dominant. Elements overwhelm the view.

B2 Lighting scenario expert

Whether the historic logic of the natural lighting of the site has survived: whether shadow and light work as originally intended (window openings, orientation of the façade, shading elements), or this has been disrupted by later changes.

5Exceptional. The scenario is fully preserved, shadows and patches of light work as the original spatial instrument.
4High. Historically correct, minimal deviations (one replaced window, an altered shading element).
3Neutral. Partly preserved, with substantial changes to the lighting regime.
2Degraded. Substantial changes to the original lighting scenario.
1Lost. The lighting regime is entirely changed, the original intent cannot be read.

C1 Tactile quality expert

How far the surface of the material conveys tactile information about its authenticity (assessed visually, by texture).

5Exceptional. Complex natural texture (rough limestone, aged wood, patinated bronze).
4High. Textured surface, clear material identity (brick, raw concrete, terracotta).
3Moderate. Recognizable texture, limited depth (smooth concrete, aged plaster).
2Low. Flat or synthetic surface (painted metal, modern plaster).
1Absent. Synthetic material with no tactile response (PVC, anodized aluminium, plastic).

C2 Olfactory character expert

Whether the site is linked with the characteristic smell of old material (damp stone, old wood, mineral dampness), as opposed to a neutral or synthetic smell. It is not always applicable: then the neutral score is given. From the street, behind a fence, smell cannot be assessed, so at level B give the neutral score 3 (section 7).

5Exceptional historic materiality. Earth, moss, old stone and wood, mineral dampness.
4High. The characteristic smell of the material, with no synthetic admixture.
3Neutral. No smell, or barely perceptible.
2Weak. A barely perceptible synthetic or chemical component is present.
1Synthetic or chemical. A non-historic smell predominates.

AD Type of material and Authenticity Density calculated

The value is determined by choosing a category of material, each with a fixed numerical value, with no five-point scale.

AStone, wood, Corten steel, concrete with marble aggregate. AD ≈ 0.80.
BTerracotta, patinated metal, fired brick. AD ≈ 0.65.
CPainted plaster, standard glazing. AD ≈ 0.40.
DPVC, composite panels, mirrored and tinted glass, plastic cladding, siding. AD ≈ 0.15.

Why D is worse than C. Plaster and ordinary glazing of type C lie on a historic base or stand in historic openings. They can be removed, repaired, applied again, and the base beneath survives. A type D material replaces the base with a factory system. It does not gain patina: a panel fades or cracks, and is repaired only by replacing it whole. Mirrored glass also reflects the surroundings, and the material of the façade can no longer be read.

Check on site: look at an edge, a chip or a joint. Under plaster there is a mineral layer, and under it stone or brick. A type D panel shows fixings, a flat seam and the same colour through its whole thickness. Behind a fence the chip and joint are often out of reach: if you cannot determine the material, note this in the notes field.

If the materials are mixed, choose the category by the material that dominates the façade. If the exact value is known from the design documentation, enter it separately: it takes priority over the approximate value by category.

II Integrity Index expert

It is assessed directly as a number from 0 to 100, not on a five-point scale. The reference points match the answer options in the form; for intermediate values, interpolate or enter the exact value in the separate field of the form.

90Exceptional All historic layers clearly readable.
75High History is readable, minor irreversible changes.
60Moderate Layers fragmented but present.
45Low Significant fragmentation.
25Critical Historic continuity almost lost.
10Lost Historic layering cannot be read.
The scale is provisional. In the first phase of the study exactly these anchors are used. The version of the scale is recorded together with your score. If the scale has to be refined after the first phase, it will be done centrally, and your scores will remain comparable.

Photographs

Three mandatory photographs per site. Shoot in daylight, avoiding direct glare where possible.

  1. The whole façade.
  2. A close-up detail of the material: the surface on which you based PMI and C1.
  3. Context: a shot from the street in which the site is visible together with its surroundings. Stand at the viewpoint, hold the phone at eye level and shoot horizontally. In the frame: the whole building, one or two neighbouring houses on each side, the street or pavement, the fence, greenery, wires and signs. The shot is mandatory at any access level, including A, because it is used to compare the score from “a distance” with the score from “close up”.
If the view is blocked. If the site is partly hidden by scaffolding, a parked vehicle or greenery, wait until the view clears, or make a note of it in the researcher's notes field.

Access levels A, B, C

In the study zone almost all houses stand on fenced private plots, and you will not be able to get close. So every measurement is marked with an access level: a letter showing how exactly you saw the site. The level describes how the site is seen. It is a mark of the reliability of the value, not an assessment of your work. In the register the index will be shown with the level, for example “0.62 (B)”.

A
Close up You examined the material and patina close up (including from the pavement, if you came right up to it) or the owner allowed entry to the plot. In the study zone this is the exception.
B
From public space The façade is seen from the street, a main road or the waterfront, over the fence or through the railing. The main case for the study zone.
C
Only from images and archive Orthophoto, drone images, archive. You do not need this level in the field: it is set by the organizer.

Rules in the field

  1. Set the level by what you actually saw during the measurement.
  2. A photo through or over a fence does not give level A: the surface was not examined close up. Set B.
  3. If the owner let you in, set A. Still take the three mandatory photos, including the general view from the street: it is used to compare the score from “a distance” with the score from “close up”.
  4. Do not enter someone's plot without the owner's permission, even if the gate is open. Do not ask or persuade: level B gives a full result.
  5. If the owner objects, tell the coordinator. The site gets the mark “not surveyed: owner's objection” in the register. No measurement is made, nobody enters the plot, no drone flies over the site.

What changes at level B

Parameters

  1. A1 and C2 cannot be assessed from the street: give the neutral score 3 for both.
  2. Assess PMI, C1 and AD by what is visible from the street. For surfaces of types C and D PMI equals 3 at level B too.
  3. Assess B2 and VQI from the main viewpoint. Assess II by what is visible from the street, and note in the notes that the plot and courtyard are not visible.
  4. If the material cannot be determined from the street, write this in the notes field.

Drones and plot owners

Students do not fly drones, including their own. Aerial photography is done by the organizer on a schedule and only over sites whose owners have not objected.

A leaflet about our work is handed to owners or put in their letterbox 5–7 days before the survey. Leaflets are not left on gates or façades.

What this is for

Level A sites form the calibration group: they show how far the score from “a distance” (B) differs from the score from “close up”. This gives an honest range of uncertainty for the other sites in the zone.

One level per site. The level is shown as a single letter and is not averaged. If one researcher did not get onto the plot, the others usually will not either, so the three submissions almost always agree on the level. If they nevertheless differ (the owner let one in), the coordinator settles the level.

Three independent measurements

Each site in the study zone is measured by three researchers, completely independently and blind. This is deliberate: if three independent views agree, the result can be trusted, and if they differ, that is also important information.

What you do. Give your scores on your own. Do not consult or cross-check with other researchers, even if you meet at the same site. Your task is to give your own score honestly.

Parameters marked E (PMI, VQI and its three components, B2, C1, C2, II) call for expert judgement. Rely on the scale descriptors: they are written so that different people read the same façade in the same way.

What the system does

  1. The register always holds the current average over the number of submissions that have already arrived (one, two or three). Submissions may arrive on different days.
  2. Each parameter is averaged separately (six PI parameters, AD, II), and the Chronity Index (CI) is calculated once from the averaged values. The mean of three finished indices is not taken.
  3. If submissions have different access levels, A1 and C2 are averaged only over the submissions in which they were actually assessed. The neutral three of level B is not included in the average.
  4. The protection status is not averaged: it is taken from the first submission.
  5. Each researcher's original scores are stored separately.
  6. If the difference between submissions is too large, the site is flagged for clarification, and the coordinator may contact one of you. This is a normal part of the work: the descriptors are refined, the result is not forced.

Submitting data

The completed form is sent through the field app at the direct address the organizer gives you before the trip (or through the QR code on the ATRC Field Card). After submission, the Chronity Index for the site is calculated automatically from all submissions received, and you can see the result on the site page (the site's Chrono-Passport) if you have access to it.

What happens to the data next

After all sites in the zone have been collected, the data are combined by the organizers into an overall assessment of the zone. The site's Chrono-Passport (the page with the measurement history of a specific building) works as a living page, not a one-off document: at a repeat measurement in the future the history is added to.

Direction of development. In the longer term the Chrono-Passport is planned to be taught to compare the actual changes of a site with a previously set scenario of the assessment of the impact of interventions over time (TIA, Temporal & Interventional Impact Assessment) and to signal when reality has diverged from the forecast. This is a declared direction for the development of the platform. It is not implemented in the current version of the tool.

If something does not add up

If a site does not fit any of the categories described, if the form does not accept the data, if the plot owner objects, or if you have doubts about a specific score, record the question in the researcher's notes field and tell the study coordinator as soon as possible, without waiting until the end of the day of data collection.

Rule. It is better to clarify one parameter on site than to recalculate a score afterwards from photographs.

Protocol step by step

Now that it is clear what is measured and why, go through the steps in order.

Before you leave STEPS 01–03 · OFFICE

  1. Coordinator's briefing. The list of sites for today, Zone_ID, link/QR code to the form, contact for urgent questions.
  2. Check the phone. Charge, internet access, location services switched on.
  3. Your name in the form. Enter your name or researcher code in the form field: the system uses it to tell apart the three researchers of one site.

On site, before measuring STEPS 04–06 · ON SITE

  1. Viewpoint. Find a point in public space from which the façade can be read as a whole, with no obstacles in the foreground.
  2. Check Zone_ID. The ID filled into the form must match what the coordinator named.
  3. Protection status and access level. Find out the status (section 6) if it is not known. Then determine the access level A, B or C (section 7) and mark it in the form.

Measurement STEPS 07–11 · 8 PARAMETERS

  1. Inspect the site. If the plot is accessible (level A), walk round the site along its perimeter. If it is fenced (level B), view it from the street from several points. In both cases do not enter someone's plot without the owner's permission.
  2. PI parameters. Six parameters (section 5) in any order. Work on your own: do not consult other researchers, even if you meet at the same site.
  3. Material — AD. Determine the category by the dominant material of the façade.
  4. Integrity — II. Assess against the scale anchors (section 5), taking into account which elements of the façade have survived and in what condition. In the first phase the scale is provisional: the version of the scale is recorded with the score.
  5. Photographs. Three mandatory photographs (section 9): 1) the whole façade; 2) a close-up detail of the material; 3) context: a shot from the street where the building is seen together with neighbouring houses, the street, the fence and greenery.

After measuring STEPS 12–13 · CHECK

  1. Check the form. All required fields, access level, GPS found, photographs attached.
  2. Submit. Send your form without waiting for the other researchers and without cross-checking with them. The Chronity Index is calculated automatically.

Questions and answers

The plot is fenced and I cannot get close. How do I score?

View the site from the street from several points, take the three photos and set level B. A photo over a fence or through a railing always gives B. This is a full result.

The owner came out and let me onto the plot.

Set level A, examine the material and patina close up, but still take the general view from the street.

The owner objects.

Tell the coordinator. The site gets the mark “not surveyed: owner's objection” in the register: no measurement is made, nobody enters the plot, no drone flies over the site. Do not persuade.

May I fly my own drone?

No. Students do not fly drones. Aerial photography is done by the organizer on a schedule and only over sites whose owners have not objected.

How do I score sound and smell if I am standing behind a fence?

At level B give the neutral score 3 for A1 and C2. They cannot be assessed from the street.

The façade is plastered or has panels, and no patina is visible.

For surfaces of types C and D PMI is always 3. The type is determined by the material of the façade (see AD).

I cannot determine the material from the street.

Choose the category by the dominant material that you can see. If you cannot determine it, write this in the notes field.

I met another researcher at the same site. May we compare scores?

No. Each gives scores on their own. A difference between researchers is not a mistake: the system averages the scores and flags the site for clarification if the difference is too large.

The form does not accept the data, or the site fits no category.

Write it in the notes field and tell the coordinator at once. Do not score afterwards from photographs.

What do I say if asked what I am doing?

The study of buildings in the Dobrota zone is carried out under a single protocol with the consent of the Municipality of Kotor (act of 18 October 2026). The coordinator's contact is on the participant card.
